# Junzi ethic — deep research study prompt

Use this prompt with a deep research tool (ChatGPT Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity Pro, Claude). The output stress-tests and refines a personal Junzi study-practice template and informs the design of a recurring AI-assisted study skill.

---

## Expert lens

Adopt the perspective of a scholar-practitioner with dual fluency: a sinologist trained in classical Confucian textual scholarship (Analects, Mencius, Xunzi, Song-Ming neo-Confucian commentaries; familiar with Slingerland, Ames & Rosemont, Van Norden, Ivanhoe, Tu Weiming) **and** an experienced contemplative-practice designer who has built sustainable daily formation practices in adjacent traditions (Stoic *prosochē*, Ignatian examen, lectio divina, Zen koan study, Mussar). The researcher should be able to evaluate both **textual fidelity** (does this honour the source tradition?) and **practice viability** (does this actually train character over months and years?).

---

## Research question

Is the attached "Junzi ethic — study practice" template a sound, well-designed personal study-and-cultivation practice — and what specific changes would make it more textually faithful, more behaviourally effective, and more sustainable as a daily/weekly rhythm? Secondarily, what design principles from comparable formation practices should shape a software tool (a recurring AI-assisted "skill") that supports this practice over the long run?

The answer should be specific enough to drive concrete edits to the template and concrete design decisions for the tool.

## Scope constraints

- **Tradition focus:** Classical Confucianism centred on the *Analects*, with appropriate use of Mencius, Xunzi, and major commentarial traditions (Zhu Xi, Wang Yangming) where relevant. Not a survey of "Eastern philosophy."
- **Practitioner profile:** A reflective adult, not a credentialed scholar, practising privately in English with one primary translation. Roughly 15–25 min/day plus a weekly review. Goal is character formation that shows up in speech, relationships, and work — not academic mastery.
- **Comparator traditions:** Limit comparison to formation practices with substantial written practitioner literature: Stoicism (esp. Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, modern Stoa Nova), Ignatian examen, lectio divina, Mussar (esp. Cheshbon HaNefesh / Salanter tradition), and Zen koan study. Briefly note Buddhist *sīla* / *vipassanā* if directly illuminating. Exclude pop self-help, "stoicism for bros," and generic mindfulness content.
- **Evidence standard:** Prefer primary texts, peer-reviewed scholarship, and serious practitioner literature. Trade-press and SEO content only as last resort, flagged.
- **Exclude:** Political-Confucianism debates, corporate "Confucian leadership" branding, modern state ideology, unless directly relevant to the personal-practice question.

## Phases

### Phase 1 — Textual-fidelity scan
**Question this phase answers:** Does the template's framing of *junzi* and its practice domains accurately reflect the *Analects* and mainstream Confucian scholarship, or does it distort, flatten, or anachronise key concepts?

Sub-questions:
- Are the practice domains a defensible map of *junzi* conduct as portrayed in the *Analects*? What is missing, over-weighted, or mis-categorised?
- Are the template's readings of *li* and *yi* textually defensible? Where do major translators / commentators disagree?
- Is the core vocabulary list complete for a personal-conduct practice, or are load-bearing terms missing (e.g. *xiao*, *zhong*, *jing*, *cheng*, *de*)?
- How does the chosen translation's interpretive frame compare to Ames & Rosemont, Lau, Van Norden, Brooks & Brooks? Where does translation choice materially change practice?
- Is the template's compression of the junzi ethic a fair representation, or does it import modern therapeutic categories?

### Phase 2 — Practice-design scan
**Question this phase answers:** Judged as a contemplative formation practice, is the template's daily protocol and weekly review well-designed for character change over 6–24 months?

Sub-questions:
- Is the daily reading-and-writing load sound given comparable practices? What does evidence from Stoic / Ignatian / Mussar literature suggest about sustainable daily volume?
- Is an outer/inner two-lane practice distinction supported by the Confucian tradition itself (e.g. *Daxue*'s inner/outer cultivation), and is it workable as a design?
- How does the weekly review template compare to the Ignatian examen, Stoic evening review, and Mussar accounting-of-the-soul? What is missing or over-engineered?
- Is front-loading outer-lane work before inner-lane work wise sequencing? What do comparator traditions do?
- What well-documented failure modes from comparable practices should be added to the pitfalls list — e.g. spiritual bypassing, scrupulosity, ritualism, hyper-individuation?

### Phase 3 — Comparator deep-dive (per-tradition)

### Phase 4 — Synthesis
**Question this phase answers:** What patterns hold across these traditions about what makes a personal formation practice work over years, and where does the Junzi template align or diverge?

Address:
- Cross-traditional convergences on practice design (small daily volume, stable form, periodic review, embodied test, written record).
- Genuine tensions between Confucian formation and the comparator practices (e.g. role of relationship and ritual vs interior individual focus; absence of metaphysical-soteriological frame in early Confucianism vs presence in Christian/Buddhist comparators).
- Non-obvious risks specific to a *junzi* practice undertaken privately in English without a teacher, lineage, ritual community, or source-language access — and how to mitigate them.
- Whether the template's emphases reflect the tradition's centre of gravity or the practitioner's modern preoccupations — and whether that matters.

### Phase 5 — Recommendations for the template
**Question this phase answers:** What concrete, prioritised edits should the practitioner make?

Output:
- **Keep as-is:** sections / framings that are sound.
- **Revise:** specific sections with proposed replacement language or structural changes; explain why.
- **Add:** missing elements (concepts, daily/weekly mechanics, pitfalls, vocabulary).
- **Cut or de-emphasise:** anything that risks distortion, over-engineering, or unsustainable load.
- **Open questions** the practitioner should sit with rather than resolve up front.

### Phase 6 — Recommendations for the supporting tool
**Question this phase answers:** What should an AI-assisted recurring study skill *do* and *not do*?

Address:
- What roles should the AI legitimately play (passage selection? glossary? Socratic questioning? weekly-review facilitation? long-arc pattern detection)? What roles should it explicitly refuse (judging the practitioner's character, generating "junzi answers," substituting for self-examination)?
- What interaction shape best fits a formation practice? Hard gates? One-step-at-a-time? Silent capture? Structured templates vs open prose?
- What data should the skill keep across sessions, and what should it deliberately *not* persist?
- Specific failure modes to design against: gamification of virtue, scrupulosity loops, the AI flattering the practitioner's self-image, the AI imposing a Western-therapeutic frame on Confucian categories.
- 5–8 concrete design rules, each tied to a finding from earlier phases.

## Source priority

**Confucian primary texts and translations:**
- *Analects* — Slingerland (Hackett), Ames & Rosemont (Ballantine), Lau (Penguin), Brooks & Brooks (*Original Analects*), Watson (Columbia), Legge.
- *Mencius* — Van Norden, Lau. *Xunzi* — Hutton (Princeton). *Daxue / Zhongyong* — Gardner, Plaks, Tu Weiming.
- Commentarial tradition: Zhu Xi (Gardner), Wang Yangming (*Instructions for Practical Living*, Chan tr.).

**Confucian secondary scholarship:**
- Tu Weiming, Roger Ames, Henry Rosemont, Bryan Van Norden, Philip J. Ivanhoe, Edward Slingerland, Stephen Angle, Michael Puett, Erin Cline.
- Journals: *Philosophy East and West*, *Dao: A Journal of Comparative Philosophy*, *Journal of Confucian Philosophy and Culture*.

**Comparator-tradition primary and practitioner literature:**
- Stoicism: Epictetus (*Discourses*, *Enchiridion*), Marcus Aurelius, Seneca; Pierre Hadot (*Philosophy as a Way of Life*), A.A. Long, Massimo Pigliucci, John Sellars.
- Ignatian: *Spiritual Exercises*, Timothy Gallagher (*The Examen Prayer*), George Aschenbrenner.
- Lectio divina: Guigo II, Enzo Bianchi, Michael Casey.
- Mussar: *Cheshbon HaNefesh* (Mendel of Satanov), Alan Morinis, Ira Stone.
- Zen: *Mumonkan*, *Hekiganroku*; Ruth Fuller Sasaki, Victor Sōgen Hori (*Zen Sand*).

**Adjacent academic literature:**
- Hadot, Foucault (technologies of the self), MacIntyre (*After Virtue*), Annas (*Intelligent Virtue*).

**Avoid:** SEO blog summaries; "Confucianism in 5 minutes" content; corporate-leadership Confucianism; pop-Stoicism; generic mindfulness; AI-generated explainers.

**Always:** Quote named scholars/practitioners. Where the literature is thin or contested, say so explicitly.
