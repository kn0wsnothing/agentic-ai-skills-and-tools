# Deep Research Prompt: How to Use NotebookLM Effectively

## Objective
Conduct a deep research study on **best practices for using and prompting NotebookLM** to uncover meaningful connections among unstructured data and long documents.
The goal is to identify **how journalists and researchers can best utilize NotebookLM** to extract, synthesize, and cite insights across large corpora — including proven prompting strategies, workflows, and limitations.

## Context and background
- Target users: journalists, researchers, and analysts working with large document collections (e.g., reports, transcripts, datasets).
- Research aim: to understand **how NotebookLM performs when connecting ideas, references, or narratives** across many documents or long-form text sources.
- Investigate:
  - NotebookLM's architectural strengths and constraints (context length, summarization style, source citation behavior).
  - Reported workflows or prompt patterns that improve synthesis and citation reliability.
  - Situations where NotebookLM struggles (e.g., implicit connections, inconsistent source grounding).
- Identify emerging *best practices* through case studies, expert commentary, technical documentation, or user experiments.

## Step-by-step guidance
1. **Clarify the research scope**
   - Review Google's official materials, academic or newsroom uses, and trusted external analyses.
   - Define what "uncovering connections" means in practice (semantic links, narrative patterns, timelines, references).
   - Focus on multi-document or long-context use cases.

2. **Map use cases and methods**
   - Identify how journalists and analysts currently integrate NotebookLM into workflows (story discovery, synthesis, archival analysis).
   - Compare to other LLM tools for long-document analysis.

3. **Analyze prompting strategies**
   - Gather examples of how users phrase prompts to direct NotebookLM toward:
     - Cross-document reasoning ("what do these reports agree on?")
     - Relationship discovery ("where are these entities connected?")
     - Citation accuracy ("which paragraph contains this claim?")
   - Identify phrasing patterns that consistently yield coherent, sourced outputs.

4. **Evaluate technical and practical boundaries**
   - Explore model constraints (document token limits, snippet length, grounding behavior).
   - Assess user-reported limitations (citation integrity, context loss, factual drift).

5. **Extract best practices**
   - Summarize effective prompt structures and techniques, organized by task type.
   - Include workflow-level advice: document preparation, dataset organization, prompt iteration, and verification methods.

6. **Develop guidance for journalists**
   - Translate technical findings into practical newsroom workflows:
     - How to segment documents effectively
     - How to cross-check NotebookLM citations
     - How to turn NotebookLM outputs into verified leads

## Desired output format
Organize the findings as follows:
1. Executive summary
2. NotebookLM capabilities overview
3. Documented prompting techniques and examples
4. Observed failure modes and limitations
5. Proven workflows for journalists and researchers
6. Best-practice recommendations
7. Prompt templates and phrasing patterns
8. References and citations

## Proven techniques to apply in this deep research
- Use **chain-of-thought reasoning** to analyze source workflows step by step.
- Compare *what works* in NotebookLM vs. other RAG-like systems.
- Emphasize **citations, reproducibility, and grounded evidence**.

## Output requirements
Each claim or practice recommendation must:
- Include a citation or attribution (e.g., documentation URL, case study, or credible tutorial).
- Indicate whether the evidence is firsthand (NotebookLM docs/experiments) or secondary (user reports, test cases).
- Highlight where consistency or reproducibility may vary.
